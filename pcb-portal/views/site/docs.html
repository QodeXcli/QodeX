Quick start

  1. Create an account.
  2. Go to New order and drop your .kicad_pcb. Layers, size, nets and pads are extracted in the browser and a 3D preview appears.
  3. Choose the service, fab house and options. The price updates live.
  4. Place the order and pay by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or PayPal.
  5. Follow progress and chat with your engineer on the order page. Deliverables appear there with a 3D preview.

Preparing your board

  • Placement is final. Every footprint should be placed and oriented. We do not move parts.
  • Outline on Edge.Cuts. A closed outline; cut-outs and slots as closed shapes inside it.
  • Lock what must not change. Locked footprints and tracks are preserved exactly.
  • Run DRC first. Unconnected-items warnings are expected; clearance errors on existing copper are not.
  • Pre-route what you care about. Hand-routed tracks you leave in place are kept; we route the rest.

Net classes & differential pairs

Define net classes in Board Setup → Net Classes with track width, clearance and via sizes. Differential pairs are detected from KiCad naming (USB_D+/USB_D-, ETH_TX_P/ETH_TX_N) and routed with the class's pair width and gap.

Stackup & impedance

Impedance is solved from the stackup in Board Setup → Physical Stackup: dielectric thickness and εr per layer, copper thickness and finish. If you leave it at KiCad's defaults, we use the chosen fab's standard stackup for that layer count and tell you in the dossier.

Tip: state targets explicitly in the notes, e.g. USB: 90 Ω diff ±10 %, RF_*: 50 Ω SE, grounded coplanar on F.Cu.

Writing order notes

Good notes are short and specific. Examples:

DDR4: byte lanes matched ±5 mil to DQS, addr/cmd fly-by ±25 mil
PCIe x4: 85 Ω diff, max 2 layer changes per lane
VIN_48V / SW: 12 A, keep on F.Cu + In2, 2 oz outer copper
Keep-out: nothing under U7 (crystal) on In1

DFM academy · Acid traps

An acid trap is an acute angle (less than 90°) between two copper features. During etching, etchant pools in the narrow corner and over-etches it, which can open the trace or leave it thin. The fix is geometric: join tracks at 90° or wider, and mitre or arc bends.

Annular ring

The annular ring is the copper left around a drilled hole: (pad diameter − drill) / 2. Drill wander can break out of a ring that is too small. Fabs publish a minimum (commonly 0.1–0.15 mm on standard service); we check every via and pad against the selected fab's value.

Teardrops

A teardrop is a tapered fillet where a track meets a pad or via. It adds copper where drill wander or thermal stress would otherwise crack a narrow neck, and improves yield on thin tracks and flex regions.

Return paths

Every signal current returns through the nearest reference plane, directly beneath the trace at high frequency. If the plane has a split or void under the trace, the return current detours, creating a loop antenna and an impedance discontinuity. That is why QodeX treats plane continuity under constrained nets as a routing constraint.
